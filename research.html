Research

My work currently centres on quantum error correction. My PhD research concerned topological quantum matter, with a particular emphasis on multiband topology and Euler-class phases.

Research interests

  • Quantum error correctionStructural constraints on quantum codes, fault-tolerant gates, code automorphisms and low-overhead logical operations.
  • Multiband topologyTopological invariants that only appear when several energy gaps or bands are treated together, including Euler and non-Abelian topology.
  • Quantum geometryHow geometric properties of Bloch states constrain measurable transport and optical responses.
  • Complex and temporal networksMethods for extracting time-dependent phases or states from systems whose interactions evolve in time.

  1. 2026

    Constraints on phantom codes from automorphism group bounds

    A. S. Morris, D. Malz · arXiv:2604.15111 · accepted in Physical Review Letters

    Phantom codes make a very large family of logical CNOT operations unusually simple: they can be implemented just by permuting physical qubits.

    More detailsHide details

    We show that this simplicity imposes a sharp price on the encoding rate. Except for one special case, a phantom code encoding \(k\) logical qubits into \(n\) physical qubits must satisfy \(k \leq \log_2(n+1)\). The proof turns a fault-tolerant gate question into a statement about the symmetry, or automorphism group, of the code. In short, asking a code to possess an exceptionally large logical gate symmetry severely restricts how efficiently it can store quantum information.

  2. 2025

    Euler topology in classical spin liquids

    L. Rüegg, A. S. Morris, H. Yan, R.-J. Slager · arXiv:2505.09683

    Classical spin liquids can have band-like collective fluctuations, and those bands can carry the same kind of multiband Euler topology that appears in electronic systems.

    More detailsHide details

    We extend the topological classification of classical spin liquids to situations where several neighbouring gaps matter simultaneously. A key result is that moving — or “braiding” — band nodes around one another can change the Euler topology. That change is reflected in the characteristic pinch-point patterns of the spin structure factor, so an abstract topological process in momentum space can alter an experimentally relevant correlation pattern.

  3. 2025

    Optical manifestations and bounds of topological Euler class

    W. J. Jankowski, A. S. Morris, A. Bouhon, F. N. Ünal, R.-J. Slager · Physical Review B 111, L081103

    A non-zero Euler class constrains how strongly a material can respond to light, providing a measurable consequence of a multiband topological invariant.

    More detailsHide details

    The underlying reason is quantum geometry: the way Bloch states change as momentum changes cannot be arbitrarily small in a topological phase. This produces lower bounds on integrated optical weight and constraints involving nearby band gaps. We also show how momentum- and frequency-resolved optical measurements can reconstruct the geometric quantities entering the Euler invariant, giving a route from light absorption to a direct diagnosis of multiband topology.

  4. 2024

    Non-Abelian Hopf-Euler insulators

    W. J. Jankowski*, A. S. Morris*, Z. Davoyan, A. Bouhon, F. N. Ünal, R.-J. Slager · Physical Review B 110, 075135 · *equal contribution

    This work constructs three-band topological phases in three dimensions in which Hopf and Euler topology coexist rather than appearing as separate phenomena.

    More detailsHide details

    The bulk carries a Hopf invariant, which can be pictured in terms of linked structures in momentum space, while two-dimensional slices can carry Euler topology. In the gapless version, this combination naturally produces helical nodal structures. Opening an additional gap leads to a fully gapped multiband “flag” phase. We connect the abstract invariants to possible signatures including nonlinear optical response, unusual behaviour of Wannier functions, and non-trivial topology confined to sample surfaces.

  5. 2024

    Andreev reflection in Euler materials

    A. S. Morris, A. Bouhon, R.-J. Slager · New Journal of Physics 26, 023014

    At a superconductor interface, Euler topology produces a striking even-versus-odd dependence in Andreev reflection.

    More detailsHide details

    Andreev reflection occurs when an incoming electron is reflected as a hole while a Cooper pair enters a superconductor. We ask how this process changes when the normal material has an Euler-type band crossing with winding number \(n\). The reflection probabilities depend qualitatively on whether \(n\) is even or odd, and the differential conductance can be strongly suppressed for even winding. This suggests a transport route for inferring a topological winding number from a superconducting junction.

  6. 2023

    Phasik: a Python package to identify system states in partially temporal networks

    M. Lucas, A. Townsend-Teague, M. Neri, S. Poetto, A. S. Morris, B. Habermann, L. Tichit · Journal of Open Source Software 8(91), 5872

    Phasik is a Python package for finding recurring states or phases in networks when only part of the network has explicit time-dependent information.

    More detailsHide details

    Many complex systems are networks whose connections change with time, but real datasets often record the time dependence of only some interactions. Phasik helps construct temporal networks, compare network snapshots and cluster similar periods together to identify system states. The main contribution is practical: methods developed for analysing temporal organisation are packaged into a reusable, documented tool that interoperates with the scientific Python ecosystem and can be applied well beyond the original biological examples.

  7. 2023

    Inferring cell cycle phases from a partially temporal network of protein interactions

    M. Lucas, A. S. Morris, A. Townsend-Teague, L. Tichit, B. Habermann, A. Barrat · Cell Reports Methods 3, 100397

    We infer biological phases from changing interaction networks rather than assuming the phase labels in advance.

    More detailsHide details

    We combine a static protein-interaction network with time-series measurements to create a sequence of time-dependent network snapshots. Clustering those snapshots recovers familiar phases and sub-phases of the budding-yeast cell cycle, and the same framework also detects altered dynamics in mutants and circadian-rhythm data. The broader idea is that temporal phases can emerge from the changing structure of interactions themselves, even when only part of the network has explicit timing information.
